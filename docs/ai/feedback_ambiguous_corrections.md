# Ambiguous corrections

> When a short correction could contradict a very recent explicit decision, confirm which thing to change before editing. Don't guess.

Carried over from ramprate-ui (2026-07-08): minutes after the owner explicitly approved repointing
4 CTA buttons, a short, rough message ("don't change any where and don't replase contact") seemed to
reverse it, but could equally have meant "don't touch the /contact page itself." Asking a concrete
question that listed the exact 4 CTAs and their old/new links settled it; reverting without asking
would have been a coin flip with real cost either way.

**Why:** guessing wrong costs trust in both directions: undoing work the person wanted kept, or
keeping a change they asked to undo.

**How to apply:** when a terse or informal message could contradict something the person decided
very recently (especially through a structured question/answer), don't infer intent from grammar.
Ask one options-based question that names the specific earlier change before editing or
reverting. The bar for asking is lower when reverting recent explicit work is on the table.
